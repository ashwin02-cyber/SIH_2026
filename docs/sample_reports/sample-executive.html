Recommended actions

PriorityAction
HighReplace the weak Diffie-Hellman group. Change the key-exchange group from Group 2 (1024-bit MODP) to an elliptic-curve group (19 or 20) or MODP 3072-bit or larger.
MediumPrefer an AEAD cipher (AES-256-GCM) over AES-CBC. Use aes256gcm16 for IKE and ESP: it protects integrity and confidentiality in one step and needs no separate HMAC.
MediumVerify that Perfect Forward Secrecy is enabled. PFS is negotiated inside encrypted IKE messages and cannot be seen in a capture. Confirm the ESP proposal contains a DH group (for strongSwan: esp=...-ecp384!) with `ipsec statusall`.
MediumCapture from before the tunnel comes up. Start the capture, then bring the tunnel up (for example `tcpdump -w` first, then `ipsec up`), so IKE_SA_INIT is included.
MediumReduce the traffic-type leak. An eavesdropper can tell this traffic is web browsing (100%). Padding packets to fixed sizes and adding dummy traffic reduces this; see the defence simulation for the effect measured on this project's dataset.
LowUse tunnel mode unless host-to-host transport mode is required. Tunnel mode encrypts the original IP header and hides the real endpoints behind the gateways.
LowSet and verify a short SA lifetime. Set keylife=1h and ikelifetime=4h, then verify with a capture at least twice as long as keylife so a rekey is observed.

What this report cannot tell you

  • An IPsec capture only shows the cipher and key-exchange group when the start of the VPN negotiation was captured. Whether forward secrecy is on is negotiated in an encrypted step and is not visible to an observer. Tunnel versus transport mode and the cipher family (CBC or GCM) can only be estimated statistically from packet sizes, and are marked "inferred" with a confidence.
  • The traffic-type label is a statistical guess from packet sizes and timing, trained on five scripted traffic types. It is not proof of what users did.
